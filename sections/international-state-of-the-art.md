# International State of the Art

## Benchmark Studies Comparing Sentinel-2 Time-Series Yield Models

### Systematic reviews synthesizing the field

A 2026 systematic review by Narimani, Pourreza, Moghimi and Farajpoor, "Sentinel-2 for Crop Yield Estimation: A Systematic Review" (arXiv:2603.23779, submitted 24 March 2026), synthesizes recent advances in Sentinel-2-based crop yield estimation and is the most directly on-point review identified for the HARVEST state of the art [10]. It identifies a shift from regional to field- and sub-field-scale yield assessment driven by three main methodological families: (i) empirical models combining vegetation indices with machine learning and deep learning (Random Forest, CNNs); (ii) data assimilation of Sentinel-2-derived biophysical variables (notably Leaf Area Index) into process-based crop growth models such as WOFOST and SAFY; and (iii) data fusion of Sentinel-2 optical data with Sentinel-1 SAR to mitigate cloud-related gaps in the optical time series [10]. The review concludes that ML/DL and hybrid frameworks can explain substantial within-field yield variability, but that performance is constrained by limited ground-truth data, cloud-induced gaps in the observed time series, and poor model transferability across years and locations — and it explicitly names "improved in-season observations" as a future research direction, which is directly relevant to HARVEST's premise that the unobserved future part of the season limits early prediction [10]. The review abstract text was retrieved directly; it does not, in the retrieved excerpt, enumerate the specific number of studies reviewed, per-crop breakdowns, or quantitative R²/RMSE ranges, so those details cannot be reported here.

**Confidence: HIGH | Depth: MEDIUM**

### Cross-modal and cross-model benchmark studies

Pathak et al. (Deepak Pathak, Miro Miranda, Francisco Mena, Cristhian Sanchez, Patrick Helber, Benjamin Bischke, Peter Habelitz, Hiba Najjar, Jayanth Siddamsetty, Diego Arenas, Michaela Vollmer, Marcela Charfuelan, Marlon Nuske, Andreas Dengel), "Predicting Crop Yield With Machine Learning: An Extensive Analysis Of Input Modalities And Models On a Field and sub-field Level," presented at IEEE IGARSS 2023 (arXiv:2308.08948, submitted 17 August 2023), is a peer-reviewed benchmark study that compares crop- and model-agnostic machine learning methods for sub-field-level yield prediction using Sentinel-2 imagery as the primary input modality, combined with weather, soil, and digital elevation model (DEM) data in an early-fusion framework designed for global scalability [11]. Its central finding is that the best-performing combination of input modalities is not universal but depends on region, crop, and the specific model used — directly supporting the HARVEST proposal's framing that EO-only versus EO-plus-auxiliary-data approaches (methodological categories 1 and 2 in the HARVEST comparison) trade off differently across contexts [11]. The retrieved abstract does not report specific crop types, R²/RMSE/MAE values, or spatial/temporal resolution figures, so quantitative performance cannot be cited from this source at this time.

**Confidence: HIGH | Depth: MEDIUM**
**Uncertainty:** Quantitative accuracy metrics (R², RMSE, MAE) for [11] were not present in the retrieved abstract; the full paper would need to be consulted for category-1-vs-category-2 performance deltas.

A field-scale benchmark study, "Winter wheat yield estimation at the field scale using sentinel-2 data and deep learning" (Computers and Electronics in Agriculture, 2023, DOI 10.1016/j.compag.2023.108555), compared Random Forest (RF), a standard Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), and an attention-based 1D CNN ("ACNN") for estimating winter wheat yield at county and field scale in Henan Province, China, using Sentinel-2 MSI Level-2A time series [12]. According to search-indexed snippets of the paper (direct WebFetch of the ScienceDirect page returned HTTP 403 during this research pass, so this is not a full-text-verified read), the ACNN model achieved R² = 0.44 and RMSE = 1544 kg/ha, using attention weighting over convolutional features to reduce high-yield underestimation and low-yield overestimation bias relative to RF and plain CNN baselines [12]. The study reportedly identifies the heading stage as the pivotal phenological period for yield formation and highlights spectral bands B8 (835.1 nm, NIR) and B12 (2202.4 nm, SWIR) as the most informative for yield estimation [12]. This is a useful data point for the HARVEST proposal because it quantifies a modest R² (0.44) for a purely observational, non-forecasting (category-1) deep learning approach on a single crop, which HARVEST can use as a comparison baseline for category-4 pipelines — but it should be treated as provisional pending direct access to the full text.

**Confidence: MEDIUM | Depth: LOW**
**Uncertainty:** This paper's methodology and metrics were extracted from search-engine snippets, not from direct retrieval of the article. The R²=0.44 / RMSE=1544 kg/ha figures and the "heading stage" / B8-B12 band findings should be independently verified against the published PDF before being used as a precise quantitative baseline in the funding proposal.

### Synthesis on crop coverage and methodology comparisons

Taken together, the identified benchmark and review literature confirms three consistent patterns relevant to HARVEST. First, systematic reviews (e.g., [10]) converge on a taxonomy of three non-forecasting methodological families for Sentinel-2 yield estimation — vegetation-index/ML-DL empirical models, crop-growth-model data assimilation, and Sentinel-1/Sentinel-2 data fusion — none of which explicitly forecast or reconstruct the unobserved future portion of the vegetation trajectory before predicting yield; this is consistent with, but not a substitute for, a dedicated search for category-4 (forecast-then-predict) pipelines, which should be pursued separately. Second, cross-modal benchmarking work such as [11] shows that reported performance is highly context-dependent (region, crop, model), which cautions against citing any single R²/RMSE figure as representative of "the" state of the art. Third, single-crop field-scale deep learning benchmarks such as [12] report only moderate explained variance (R² in the 0.4–0.5 range) even with attention-based deep learning on relatively rich Sentinel-2 time series, suggesting headroom that HARVEST's future-trajectory-forecasting approach could plausibly address — though this inference is not itself evidence of novelty and must be corroborated by a dedicated search for category-3/category-4 prior art.

**Confidence: MEDIUM | Depth: MEDIUM**
**Uncertainty:** None of the sources gathered in this pass directly addresses methodological category 3 (forecasting/reconstructing the future EO trajectory) or category 4 (the full HARVEST-type pipeline). This gap should be treated as "not yet searched" rather than "confirmed absent," and a targeted follow-up search on vegetation-index/EO trajectory forecasting (e.g., LSTM/Transformer/Gaussian Process extrapolation of NDVI or biophysical variable time series) is still required before any novelty claim for HARVEST can be supported.

**Coverage gap — crop, geography, and quantitative comparison.** The evidence base assembled in this pass is narrower than the section title implies. Of the four benchmark/review sources, only [12] reports quantitative accuracy figures (R², RMSE) for a direct multi-model comparison, and it is the lowest-confidence source in the set (Tier 3, snippet-only, unverified full text) and covers a single crop (winter wheat) in a single region (Henan, China). [10] and [11] are Tier 1 but their retrieved abstracts contain no per-crop or per-region quantitative breakdowns, and [13] is unverified beyond its title. No source in this pass benchmarks Sentinel-2 time-series models for corn, soybean, or rice, nor covers North America, Europe, or Africa with quantitative model-comparison data. Consequently, no cross-study quantitative comparison table (e.g., RF vs. LSTM vs. Transformer R²/RMSE by crop) can yet be constructed, and any claim about "typical" Sentinel-2 yield-model accuracy should be treated as unsupported until additional crop- and region-diverse benchmark studies with verified full-text access are located.
