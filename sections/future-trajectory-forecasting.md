# Trajectory Forecasting and Reconstruction

## Sequence-to-sequence and encoder-decoder models forecasting future NDVI/EVI values

Three studies were identified that explicitly forecast the *unobserved future portion* of a vegetation-index or multi-band Sentinel-2 time series using encoder-decoder-style deep learning architectures, as opposed to interpolating or gap-filling past/missing observations within an already-completed season.

**Iele et al. (2026), "Probabilistic NDVI Forecasting from Sparse Satellite Time Series and Weather Covariates"** (arXiv:2602.17683, Università Campus Bio-Medico di Roma, Umeå University, University of Genoa) proposes a transformer-based encoder-decoder architecture with two decoupled encoding branches — one for historical NDVI plus meteorological observations, one for future weather covariates — whose representations are fused before decoding. The decoder performs parallel multi-step quantile prediction (quantile levels 0.1, 0.5, 0.9) rather than classic autoregressive sequential decoding [22]. The forecast horizon is up to 14 days ahead, corresponding to roughly three future Sentinel-2 acquisitions given the satellite's revisit schedule [22]. Experiments use the GreenEarthNet benchmark dataset (24,061 spatio-temporal data cubes, European agricultural areas, 2017–2022, multiple Köppen–Geiger climate zones) [22]. Reported accuracy: RMSE 0.096±0.155, MAE 0.062±0.074, CRPS 0.038±0.050 (NDVI units, 0–1 scale) [22]. The paper explicitly does not use the forecasted NDVI trajectory for yield prediction or any other downstream task — the contribution is scoped strictly to the forecasting step itself [22].

**Farbo, Sarvia, De Petris, Basile, and Borgogno-Mondino (2024), "Forecasting corn NDVI through AI-based approaches using Sentinel 2 image time series"** (ISPRS Journal of Photogrammetry and Remote Sensing, vol. 211, pp. 244–261) develops a Bidirectional-LSTM-based forecasting model (reported in secondary summaries as a "2-BiLSTM" configuration) that predicts field-level corn NDVI at short-to-medium horizons of 5, 10, and 15 days ahead, using NDVI, NDWI, temperature, and precipitation as predictors, with a custom "Boosting Adaptive Time Series Slicer (BATS)" data-augmentation step and the four most recent timesteps as the most informative inputs [23]. Ablation results identify NDWI as the most important auxiliary predictor [23]. This is closer to a sliding-window sequence-to-sequence forecaster than a full encoder-decoder with a distinct latent bottleneck, but it is architecturally in the same family (recurrent encoder of the observed trajectory driving multi-step-ahead prediction of the unobserved trajectory). The paper does not extend the forecasted NDVI into a yield-prediction step; the stated application is agronomic decision support (irrigation, fertilization, pest control timing), not yield estimation [23].

**Zhao and Efremova (2024), "Prediction of Sentinel-2 multi-band imagery with attention BiLSTM for continuous earth surface monitoring"** (arXiv:2407.00834) uses an attention-augmented BiLSTM in a "sequence-to-one" forecasting framework (predicting one future multi-band Sentinel-2 image, including NDVI and other vegetation indices, at a user-specified future or cloud-obscured date, from a preceding short sequence) [24]. This is a step below full seq2seq (single-step rather than multi-step decoding), but it directly targets the same problem — producing values for dates beyond the last observed acquisition — and explicitly names NDVI and "multiple vegetation indices" among its predicted outputs, not just raw spectral bands [24]. The abstract available for this paper does not specify the exact forecast horizon in days, and no downstream yield-prediction use is described [24].

## Downstream use of forecasted trajectories for yield prediction

Across all three papers reviewed here, none report using the forecasted (as opposed to observed) portion of the NDVI/EVI or Sentinel-2 trajectory as an input to a subsequent crop-yield prediction model [22][23][24]. Each paper treats vegetation-trajectory forecasting as a self-contained task, evaluated against ground-truth future satellite observations rather than against a downstream agronomic outcome. This is consistent with the HARVEST project's premise that a full category-4 pipeline (partial EO trajectory → forecast/reconstruct future trajectory → completed trajectory → yield prediction) has not been demonstrated by these particular studies, though it does not rule out that such a pipeline exists elsewhere in the literature not covered by this search pass. This finding should be cross-checked against the dedicated HARVEST gap-analysis search before being treated as conclusive.

**Confidence: MEDIUM | Depth: LOW**

**Uncertainty:** This search covered three papers found via two search queries; it is not a systematic/exhaustive review of all seq2seq vegetation-forecasting literature. Absence of a category-4 pipeline in these three papers is evidence toward, but not proof of, the absence of such a pipeline in the broader literature — a dedicated, broader search (including non-arXiv/non-English venues and operational-system documentation) is needed before this can support a novelty claim in the funding proposal.
