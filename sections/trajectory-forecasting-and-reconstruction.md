# Trajectory Forecasting and Reconstruction

## Transformer-based temporal foundation models for EO time series

**Confidence: MEDIUM | Depth: MEDIUM**

A distinct and fast-growing line of work builds general-purpose "foundation models" for Earth Observation time series — large transformer or masked-autoencoder architectures pre-trained self-supervised on large volumes of multi-temporal satellite imagery, then fine-tuned or probed for downstream tasks. Three representative models were reviewed for this task: Presto, Prithvi-EO-2.0, and (as a directly relevant adjacent architecture) the diffusion-based vegetation forecaster VegeDiff. SatMAE proper could not be independently reviewed in this pass (see Uncertainty below); Prithvi-EO-2.0 explicitly uses a masked-autoencoder/ViT design in the SatMAE lineage, so it is treated here as the accessible representative of that family.

**Presto** ("Lightweight, Pre-trained Transformers for Remote Sensing Timeseries," Tseng, Cartuyvels, Zvonkov, Purohit, Rolnick, Kerner; NASA Harvest / KU Leuven / University of Maryland / Arizona State / McGill-Mila; arXiv:2304.14065, first submitted April 2023, revised February 2024, presented at a NeurIPS 2023 Climate Change AI workshop) is a lightweight pixel-timeseries transformer pre-trained via self-supervised masked autoencoding on Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2, DEM and Dynamic World pixel time series [26]. Its stated design goal is transfer learning and feature extraction for globally distributed remote-sensing tasks, with "monitoring crop growth" and crop-type mapping cited as example applications, and the pretraining objective explicitly reconstructs masked timepoints/channels, which the authors argue makes the model robust to missing data, variable temporal resolution, and short observed subsequences [26]. This reconstruction-of-masked-inputs objective is temporally different from forward forecasting: masking in Presto's pretraining is applied within an already-fully-observed historical sequence (interpolation/denoising), not to an unobserved future window appended after the last real observation. The retrievable abstract and search-index material do not state that Presto was evaluated on forward forecasting of future, not-yet-collected timesteps, nor that it was used for crop yield prediction; its confirmed downstream evaluations are classification/mapping-oriented (e.g., crop type, land cover) [26]. **Uncertainty:** this assessment is based on the abstract and secondary summaries rather than a full read of the paper's experiments section, so the possibility that a forecasting-specific probe exists in the full text (or in follow-up work using the public Presto codebase at github.com/nasaharvest/presto) cannot be ruled out.

**Prithvi-EO-2.0** ("A Versatile Multi-Temporal Foundation Model for Earth Observation Applications," ~36 co-authors including Daniela Szwarcman, Sujit Roy and Paolo Fraccaro, NASA/IBM-led with academic collaborators, arXiv:2412.02732, also published at a later IEEE venue) is a masked-autoencoder, ViT-backbone foundation model trained on 4.2 million global multi-temporal samples from NASA's Harmonized Landsat/Sentinel-2 (HLS) archive at 30 m resolution, with explicit temporal and location embeddings [27]. It is benchmarked via GEO-Bench across geospatial tasks spanning 0.1–15 m resolution and is reported to outperform the earlier Prithvi-EO model by roughly 8% and to outperform six other geospatial foundation models on comparable benchmarks [27]. The abstract and available summaries describe evaluation on disaster response, land cover/crop mapping, and ecosystem dynamics monitoring, but do not claim or describe a forward-forecasting task (predicting satellite observations or vegetation indices at future, unobserved dates); the temporal embeddings are described as improving representation of "seasonal and long-term dynamics" for tasks performed on already-collected multi-temporal stacks, not as enabling prediction beyond the last observed timestep [27]. No mention of crop yield forecasting appears in the retrievable abstract content [27].

Critically, Prithvi-EO-2.0's encoder has since been used as a component in a **downstream crop-yield regression** pipeline: **FARM** ("Fine-Tuning Geospatial Foundation Models for Intra-Field Crop Yield Regression," Nejadshamsi, Zhang, Zaki, Porth, Porth, Khoshdel, arXiv:2510.26609) fine-tunes the pre-trained Prithvi-EO-2.0-600M encoder for continuous, pixel-level (30 m) yield regression on canola in the Canadian Prairies, reporting RMSE 0.44 and R²=0.81 [28]. This is directly relevant to HARVEST's methodological categories: FARM is a category-1/2-type approach — it regresses yield from the foundation-model embedding of observed multi-temporal imagery — and its abstract gives no indication that it forecasts or reconstructs a future/unobserved part of the growing-season trajectory before predicting yield; it operates on the imagery actually available for the field in question [28]. FARM therefore demonstrates that transformer/foundation-model embeddings are being adopted for direct EO-to-yield regression (category 1), but it is not evidence of a category-4 pipeline (forecast/reconstruct future trajectory → predict yield from the completed trajectory).

As an adjacent but architecturally distinct approach, **VegeDiff** ("Latent Diffusion Model for Geospatial Vegetation Forecasting," Zhao, Chen, Zhang, Xiao, Bai, Ouyang, arXiv:2407.12592, submitted July 2024) is described in its abstract as "the first to employ a diffusion model to probabilistically capture the uncertainties in vegetation change processes," separately modelling meteorological/environmental drivers and generating "clear and accurate future vegetation states" rather than performing classification [25]. This is a genuine forward-forecasting model for vegetation trajectories, but (a) it is a latent-diffusion architecture, not a transformer/foundation-model in the Presto/Prithvi/SatMAE sense, and (b) the retrievable abstract gives no dataset, metric, or crop-yield-application detail, so its applicability to incomplete-growing-season yield forecasting could not be assessed from the material reviewed [25]. It is nonetheless relevant prior art for the "forecasting/reconstructing the future EO trajectory" methodological category (category 3) and should be treated as a comparator for HARVEST's trajectory-completion module, pending a full-text review of its data, horizon, and validation.

### Applicability to incomplete growing-season data

None of the three models reviewed were confirmed, from the material accessible in this pass, to (a) take a partial/incomplete growing-season EO trajectory as input, (b) forecast or reconstruct the remaining, not-yet-observed part of that trajectory, and (c) feed the completed trajectory into a yield-prediction step — i.e., none was confirmed to implement HARVEST's category-4 pipeline end-to-end. Presto and Prithvi-EO-2.0 are built to consume and represent time series that are already collected (with self-supervised masking used only as a training-time robustness/regularization technique on historical data), making them naturally suited as encoders for direct or multimodal yield regression (categories 1–2, as FARM demonstrates) rather than as forecasters of unobserved future vegetation states. VegeDiff is architecturally suited to category 3 (it explicitly forecasts future vegetation states) but was not found, from its abstract, to be connected to a downstream yield-prediction step or evaluated for robustness to varying degrees of season completeness (e.g., forecasting from a prediction date early vs. late in the season). No source reviewed here provides direct counter-evidence against the HARVEST category-4 novelty claim, but the search space of transformer-based foundation models is large and this review covered only three representative models plus one adjacent diffusion model; a more exhaustive review (e.g., of SatMAE proper, Clay, DOFA, and other 2024–2026 EO foundation models) would be needed before a strong novelty claim is made.

**Uncertainty:** SatMAE itself was not independently located and reviewed as a standalone source in this pass — it is referenced here only via its architectural family relationship to Prithvi-EO-2.0 (both use masked-autoencoder/ViT designs), based on a search-engine-generated summary rather than a directly fetched SatMAE paper or abstract. This should be corrected in a follow-up pass by fetching the original SatMAE paper (Cong et al., NeurIPS 2022) directly.
